# Avoid signs of AI writing

Checklist distilled from [Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing). Scan a draft against this before sending it.

## Words to cut on sight

`delve`, `boast(s)`, `tapestry`, `vibrant`, `underscore(s)`, `showcase(s)`, `realm`, `testament to`, `navigate`, `landscape`, `journey`, `foster(ing)`, `intricate/intricacies`, `notable`, `robust`, `comprehensive`, `meticulous(ly)`, `seamless(ly)`, `elevate`, `unlock`, `unleash`, `game-changer`, `cutting-edge`, `pivotal`, `holistic`, `leverage` (as a verb), `dive into`, `deep dive`, `unpack`, `paramount`, `bespoke`, `nuanced`, `additionally` (especially opening a sentence), `align with`, `bolstered`, `crucial`, `emphasizing`, `enduring`, `enhance`, `garner`, `highlight` (as a verb), `interplay`, `valuable`, `rich`, `profound`, `exemplifies`, `commitment to`, `nestled`, `groundbreaking`, `renowned`, `featuring`, `diverse array`.

## Phrases to cut on sight

- "In the world of X", "in today's fast-paced world", "in the heart of"
- "Stands as a testament to", "plays a significant/vital role in", "serves as a reminder"
- "It's important to note that", "it's worth noting", "one could argue"
- "In conclusion", "overall", "in summary" (at the end of a short answer)
- "Not only X, but also Y" (almost always cuttable to just Y, or two plain sentences)
- "It's not X, it's Y" / "no X, no Y" used as a rhetorical flourish rather than a real contrast
- "X rather than Y" tacked on to manufacture contrast where a plain statement would do
- Anything padded to a rule-of-three ("fast, reliable, and scalable") when one or two words would do
- "-ing" clauses tacked onto the end of a sentence to manufacture significance: "..., underscoring its importance", "..., highlighting the need for"
- Canned significance-inflation templates: "represents a shift", "marks a turning point", "reflects a broader trend", "sets the stage for", "an evolving landscape", "a focal point", "left an indelible mark", "deeply rooted in"
- Canned wrap-up structure: "Despite these challenges, ... faces an uncertain future" or any formulaic "Challenges and Future Outlook" closer
- Vague connection-laundering: "in connection with", "associated with", "in association with" used where a direct, specific relationship should be stated instead

## Avoidance of basic copulatives

AI prose routinely avoids a plain "is/are" in favor of an inflated stand-in: "serves as", "functions as", "operates as", "represents", "stands as". If "X is Y" says exactly what's meant, use "is". Reach for a different verb only when it adds real information the copula doesn't.

## Structural tics

- Headers, bold, and bullets on an answer short enough to read as plain prose. Structure earns its keep only past a few paragraphs or several distinct points.
- Restating the question before answering it.
- A summary paragraph that repeats what the last three paragraphs already said.
- Hedges and disclaimers that don't change what the reader can do: "as of my last update", "I may be wrong, but", apologizing for a limitation that isn't actually blocking the answer.
- False balance for its own sake: "on one hand... on the other hand" used to pad length rather than because both sides matter here.
- Vague, unattributed claims: "many believe", "it is widely recognized", "experts agree", "industry reports suggest" with no source.
- Em dashes used as a default connective. Use a period, comma, colon, or parens; pick the one that actually fits.
- Chatbot sign-off residue: "I hope this helps!", "let me know if you have questions", "certainly!" as a reply-opener.
- Document self-narration: prose that talks about the document's own structure or the writer's organizational choices instead of just stating the content — "X is introduced here, not defined elsewhere", "this section is required to delimit the meaning of...", "Section N turns Y into Z", "these six are not an arbitrary sample... chosen for...", "the items below follow directly from... rather than constituting a separate methodology". These read like an aside to the reader justifying a placement or writing decision, not like the actual claim. State the content directly; let structure (headings, forward references) carry the organization instead of narrating it.

## Formatting tics

- Boldface scattered through a paragraph for mechanical emphasis rather than to mark a genuine key term.
- Inline-header vertical lists: every bullet built as "**Bolded lead-in term**: description", used as a mechanical template rather than because each item needs a label.
- Title Case Applied To Every Word Of A Heading, instead of sentence case.
- A heading that contains only child headings and no text of its own.
- Skipping heading levels (jumping from `#` to `###`), or overusing top-level `#` headings for what should be subsections.
- Curly/smart quotation marks and apostrophes (""", "'") in a plain-text or markdown context that otherwise uses straight quotes, or the reverse inconsistency.
- Emoji used as bullet points or section markers instead of actual list/heading syntax.
- A table built for content that reads more naturally as prose or a short list.
- A horizontal-rule thematic break dropped between sections with no other structural reason.

## Quick test

Read the draft aloud. If a sentence would sound off coming from a colleague at a whiteboard, cut or rewrite it.
